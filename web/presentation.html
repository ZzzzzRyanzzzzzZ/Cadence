Cadence

Recovery has a rhythm.

A companion for the weeks after a concussion, built for Hack for Humanity.

The problem

The advice was
a dark room.

Rest, no screens, no reading, until you feel better. That is still what most people are told.

It is out of date, and it makes recovery slower.

Why it matters

This is not rare.

1M+children concussed each year in North America
15–33%still have symptoms weeks or months later
~50%reduction in persistent symptoms from early dosed exercise

CDC National Concussion Surveillance System · Zemek et al., JAMA 2016 · Leddy et al., JAMA Pediatrics 2019

The gap

The guidance changed.
The tools didn't.

You are assessed once, handed a leaflet, and sent home.

The weeks that actually decide your recovery happen at home, unmeasured.

What Cadence does

Instrumentation for that gap.

Daily check-inThe 22 item scale clinicians use, in four validated clusters
Recovery curveFitted to your data, with honest uncertainty
Screen toleranceThe interface adapts before you notice you are struggling
Six cognitive testsScored against your own baseline, not an average
Exposure ladderTurns fear of activity into a measurable number
Caregiver pageFor the parent or coach, in plain language

The model

Your curve, not
the average curve.

Exponential decay fitted by Gauss-Newton least squares. 240 bootstrap refits give an 80 percent confidence band.

Refuses to fit under 4 check-ins

The centrepiece

The screen adapts
to you.

Six passive signals: cursor steadiness, scroll reversals, backspace rate, tab switches, long pauses, time on screen.

Above the threshold it grows the text, kills the motion, flattens the background. No camera. No keystroke content. Nothing transmitted.

Fear, measured

The exposure ladder.

Guess how bad an activity will feel. Do it. Log what it actually cost.

13 of 13 attempts were easier than expected, by 2.2 points.

That closing gap is recovering confidence, made visible.
